## Headline

Indonesia Has a 6 GW Battery Storage Target. The Biggest Barrier Isn't Technology.

---

## The Post (Copy)

Indonesia's grid ambitions are bold.

PLN's RUPTL 2025–2034 targets 6 GW of battery energy storage (BESS) by 2034 — part of a 10.3 GW storage buildout to absorb surging renewable capacity.

The 320 GWh distributed storage roadmap unveiled last year? Even bolder.

But here's the uncomfortable truth most project developers won't say out loud:

**The technology isn't the bottleneck.**

The monetisation framework is.

**What's actually holding BESS back across Indonesia:**

→ No standardised revenue stacking mechanism for storage operators
→ Grid service compensation (frequency regulation, spinning reserve) remains undefined in tariff structures
→ Private capital is waiting — not because projects don't pencil, but because the revenue model doesn't exist yet
→ Remote island microgrids, where diesel replacement is most urgent, lack off-take certainty

This isn't unique to Indonesia. Across ASEAN — as the focus of this month's 4th ASEAN Battery Technology Conference (Aug 2026, Malaysia) — the conversation has shifted from *"Can we deploy BESS?"* to *"How do we make the economics work at scale?"*

Indonesia is moving. A new government regulation on BESS for remote locations is expected this year. That's a start.

But for a country that must displace diesel across 17,000 islands while hitting net-zero targets, a regulation is a floor — not a ceiling.

The real unlock? A bankable, stackable revenue model that compensates storage for every service it provides the grid: capacity, ancillary services, voltage support, and energy arbitrage.

That's how you turn 6 GW on paper into 6 GW in the ground.
