## Headline

We're building more solar than the grid can absorb. The solution might be a 100-hour rust battery.

---

## The Post (Copy)

Chile curtailed over **6 TWh** of renewable energy in 2025.

That energy was generated. It just had nowhere to go.

Without its grid-scale battery fleet, curtailment would have been **43% higher** — closer to 8 TWh wasted.

This isn't a Chile problem. It's a physics problem that follows every market past ~25% solar penetration.

And the fix the industry has been banking on — lithium-ion BESS — is running into its own ceiling.

---

**Here's what the data is telling us right now:**

- US grid-scale BESS capacity went from <2 GW in 2020 → **38+ GW by mid-2025** (EIA)
- Another **24.3 GW** is expected to come online in the US in 2026 alone — a new record
- Globally, **49.4 GW / 136.5 GWh** of grid-scale storage was commissioned in the first 9 months of 2025 (a 36% YoY jump)
- Yet most of this is **4–8 hour duration**. That's enough to shift the evening peak. It's not enough to cover a 12-hour overnight gap or a multi-day weather event.

---

**This is where it gets interesting.**

Long-duration energy storage (LDES) deployments rose **49% in 2025**, reaching 15 GWh globally (Wood Mackenzie).

And the technology leading the charge isn't what most people expected: **iron-air batteries**.

- Form Energy's iron-air system targets **100-hour discharge duration** at a commercial cost of **~$20/kWh** — a fraction of lithium-ion at equivalent duration
- The company broke ground on its first utility-scale commercial deployment with Georgia Power in 2024
- It's also building what's expected to be the **world's largest battery by energy capacity** — for a Google data center in Minnesota

That last point matters. **AI data center operators** need 24/7 dispatchable clean power. They're not willing to wait for policy signals. They're writing the purchase orders.

Data centers are becoming the demand anchor that makes long-duration storage projects bankable — faster than any renewable portfolio standard would have.

---

**The takeaway for practitioners:**

> Curtailment is no longer a theoretical grid planning concern. It's a real-time revenue leak in high-penetration markets. Short-duration BESS is a necessary but insufficient fix. The business case for 8–100 hour storage is being underwritten by an unexpected customer: hyperscalers.
